## Digital twins for repair optimization

Rotator cuff tears are among the most common musculoskeletal injuries, and surgical repairs still fail at high rates. Using patient-specific repair strategies, we might be able to counteract this problem. To show avenues toward personalized repair, I built a **digital twin of a rotator cuff tendon surrogate**. This controlled system shows the bidirectional feedback between a physical and a digital system, which allows for both actionable insights in the tendon surrogate and continuous updates in the virtual replica. Using the digital asset, I optimized suture placement in a torn tendon, with the goal of finding the optimal suture placement that would provide maximum reduction of stress concentration around the tear edge. Before and after implementing the repair, I measured displacements in the tendon surrogate. An update of boundary conditions in the virtual replica showed substantial computational-experimental agreement, compared to simulations with original assumptions. This work shows that personalized repair and improved predictions can be achieved with digital twins, and will be presented at ASME IMECE 2026 and the SES 2026 Technical Meeting.
